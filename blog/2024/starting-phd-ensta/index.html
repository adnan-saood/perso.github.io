Starting the PhD: A New Chapter at ENSTA U2IS

Created on September 15, 2024

2024   ·   PhD   doctoral   student   research   journey   ·   personal   milestones

A Moment of Clarity

Today marks a milestone: I’ve officially begun my PhD in Autonomous Systems and Robotics at ENSTA Paris, in the U2IS Laboratory (Autonomous Systems and Robotics Lab), under the supervision of Prof. Adriana Tapus.

It’s hard to overstate what this moment means to me. The PhD is the culmination of a journey that began years ago, shaped by incredible people and experiences along the way.

The Path Here

High School

A robotics club. A curious teacher who asked, “What if we built something that could move on its own?” That question stuck with me.

Engineering Degree

Four years learning the fundamentals—mathematics, physics, programming, control theory. But also: design thinking, teamwork, and the humility that comes from building something real and watching it fail.

Master’s Research at ICube Strasbourg

A turning point. I worked on blood-brain barrier opening via focused ultrasound—a problem that mattered medically, that required robotics expertise to solve, and that resulted in a patent. I learned that research isn’t just theory; it’s about solving real problems for real people.

At ICube, I met Prof. Florent Nageotte and Dr. Jonathan Vappou, who showed me what rigorous, ethical research looked like. They also connected me with Prof. Adriana Tapus, whose work on human-robot interaction and touch made me realize: this is the frontier I want to explore.

Why Touch? Why Now?

The turning point came during my Master’s thesis. I was working on robot-assisted surgery—precise, cold, technical. But as I read more about human-robot interaction, a realization hit me:

Robots can see and hear almost as well as humans, but they can barely feel.

Touch is the most intimate sense. It’s how we comfort, communicate, explore, and connect. Yet robotics has largely ignored it. This gap seemed like the most important problem I could work on.

I wanted to build robots that didn’t just look human, but that could interact with humans in genuinely empathetic ways. And touch is the gateway to that.

The PhD: Four Years of Focused Exploration

My doctoral work will center on:

  1. Tactile Sensing: Developing artificial skin systems for humanoid hands
  2. Tactile Understanding: Building machine learning models that allow robots to learn from touch
  3. Tactile Expression: Creating haptic feedback systems that help robots communicate through touch
  4. Human-Robot Interaction: Testing all of this in real interactions with humans

This is foundational work. In four years, I won’t solve touch in robotics—that’s a lifetime endeavor. But I hope to make a dent in the frontier.

Gratitude

I’m acutely aware of how fortunate I’ve been. I wouldn’t be here without:

  • My parents: For believing in education and encouraging curiosity
  • My high school robotics teacher: For that first question
  • My undergraduate mentors: For teaching me rigor and creativity
  • Prof. Nageotte & Dr. Vappou: For showing me what research integrity looks like
  • Prof. Adriana Tapus: For seeing the potential in my ideas and agreeing to mentor the PhD
  • The IP Paris doctoral school: For funding this work
  • My colleagues at U2IS: For creating an intellectually stimulating and supportive environment

This PhD is built on the shoulders of people who invested in me.

The Road Ahead

A PhD is a marathon, not a sprint. There will be moments of brilliance and moments of doubt. Experiments will fail. Papers will be rejected. Ideas I’m excited about today will seem naïve next year.

But I’m ready. I’ve been preparing for this for years, and I’m genuinely excited about the work ahead.

To Other Students Considering a PhD

If you’re at that crossroads—wondering whether to pursue doctoral research—here’s what I’d say:

A PhD is not a credential. It’s a transformation. You’ll spend years becoming an expert in a specific domain, learning to think critically, to design experiments, to communicate complex ideas, and to handle failure with grace.

It’s hard. It’s rewarding. And if you choose the right problem and the right advisor, it can change your trajectory.

For me, that problem is touch. That advisor is Adriana. And I’m ready to dive in.

What’s Next?

The plan for Year 1:

  • Build the tactile sensor array hardware
  • Collect baseline data on human hand-robot interactions
  • Publish preliminary findings
  • Begin machine learning model development

I’ll be sharing updates throughout the journey. Stay tuned.
